\section{Conclusion}
Across three audited news corpora, the tested artifact interventions remove only part of the cross-dataset gap of a TF-IDF detector: removing source markers, matching length and masking entities together removes about a quarter of the AUC gap (24.7\%), nearly all of it through length matching and entity masking, while source masking does not reduce the gap and slightly lowers transfer. The contribution of each cue depends strongly on the dataset pair: transfers involving ISOT benefit, whereas transfer between the two similarly built datasets can worsen. For two fine-tuned transformers, DistilBERT and RoBERTa, the same interventions improve transfer but no more than deleting the same number of random words, also after length matching; the size of the random-deletion effect itself differs strongly between models (RoBERTa $+0.102$ AUC, TF-IDF $-0.012$), and paired comparisons show that the models respond differently to targeted removal. Cross-dataset generalization failure should therefore not be attributed to a dataset artifact solely because removing it improves transfer; overlap-aware dataset pairing, size-matched random controls and per-pair reporting are needed to support such claims.
